## προσεάω.

We chronicle this verb from Ac 27⁷, where Ramsay renders "as the wind did not permit our straight course onward," if only for the sake of drawing attention to the fact that it is one of the few words left in the NT of which Grimm's dictum holds good, "Not found elsewhere."

## προσεργάζομαι,

"gain besides " by working, in the NT only in Lk 19¹⁶. For the double compd. προσεξεργάζομαι, *cf.* *Syll* 538 (=⁸ 970)⁹⁸ (B.C. 288) with reference to the pillars of a temple—προσεξεργασάμενος σφονδύλους ἑκάστωι τῶν κιόνων.

## προσέρχομαι.

The semi-technical use of this verb of the approach of the worshipper to God is frequent in the LXX, and is found *septies* in Heb, twice (10²²·²³) without an obj. : *cf.* P Giss I. 20¹⁸ (beg. ii/A.D.) (= *Chrest.* I. 94) ἀβίαστος οὖσα ἑκουσίως αὐτῷ προσέρχομαι, ἵνα ἔξωι σὺν κτλ τοῦ θεοῦ ἀέλποτος.\nThe same word προσέρχομαι is used technically of an audience before or a private divine of the Emperor. See also Dio Cassius lvi. 9 τοῖς θεοῖς προσήρχετο. The verb is similarly used of approach to a court in P Oxy VIII. 1119²² (A.D. 254) προσελθὼν τῷ κρατίστῳ βουλῇ. Other exx. *cf.* προσέρχομαι c. dat. are P Oxy I. 76¹⁸ (A.D. 179) οἷς ὁδοιπορ ἡ προσερχόμενος μάρτυρι ἢ τοῖς ταύτης πλημμελήμασι, "as I have no intention of entering on his inheritance " [Edd.] (*cf.* *adire hereditatem*), and P Ryl II. 234⁴ (ii/A.D.) μέλ' ἐλθὼν δέ μου ἀπροσέλθηι, and an instance, where the suit is seen in P Oxy I. 40²¹ (ii/iii A.D.) προσήλθ[ε]τε ἐλένξαντὲς εἰσένεγκαν, "Painis appeared and said."\nNo adequate parallel has yet been found to the meaning "consent to " required by the context in 1 Tim 6³ : Field (*Notes*, p. 211) appears to favour Bentley's conjecture προσέχεται for προσέρχεται, but see Parry *ad l.* A preverbial phrase is found in a new Comic Fragment [P Berol 9941¹⁴·¹⁶]—ἑνὸς προσέρχεται (of one coming to a vacant seat at a banquet).

## προσευχή,

For a pagan instance of προσευχή in the general sense of " prayer," " supplication," we can now cite the interesting letter, BGU IV. 1080² (iii/A.D.) (= *Chrest.* I. 36⁴), in which a father congratulates his son on his marriage—τὰ τὰς εὐοδίας ἡμῖν εὐχὰς πρὸ προσευχῆς.\nExx. of προσευχή as a Jewish "place of prayer," as in Ac 16¹³ (cf. 3 Macc 7²⁰), are readily forthcoming from both the inscrr. and the papyri. From the inscrr. we may cite προσευχῆν ᾠκοδόμησεν ἡ συναγωγὴ τῶν Ἰουδαίων—a quotation from the title of the Fayûm, P Magd 35 (= *Chrest.* I. p. 80), regarding a garment deposited ἐν τῇ προσευχῇ τῶν Ἰουδαίων, to await a judicial decision as to the rightful owner. And in P Tebt I. 86⁴⁸ (late ii/B.C.) we read of a προσευχή Ἰουδαίων with a δίδμ σημείωσιν near at hand. An interesting *ex.* of this verb, which is always used of prayer to the gods or to God, is afforded by P Cairo Zen 59034 (B.C. 257). A certain Zoilus had been ordered by the god Sarapis to inform a high State official Apollonius that a temple should be erected to him.
