In view of this, and of what is stated *s.v.* σωφροσύνη, we may be allowed to refer to a striking passage in Gilbert Murray's *Rise of the Greek Epic*³, p. 26, in which σώφρων or σαόφρων, " with saving thoughts," is contrasted with ὀλοόφρων, " with destructive thoughts." " There is a way of thinking which destroys and a way which saves. The man or woman who is *sôphrôn* walks among the beauties and perils of the world, feeling the love, joy, anger, and the rest ; and through all he has that in his mind which saves.—Whom does it save ? Not him only, but, as we should say, the whole situation. It saves the imminent evil from coming to be."
