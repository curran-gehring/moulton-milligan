For the meaning ‘‘I set a trap for” rather than ‘‘I put ἃ
stumbling-block in the way of,” for this important Biblical
word, reference may be made to two recent discussions.
The first by the Rev. A. Carr appeared in his Horae Biblicae

LXX, he comes to the conclusion that the underlying thought
of enticement or temptation can hardly be dissociated from
the word. And much the same conclusion is reached by
Archdeacon Allen as the result of an independent inquiry in
his St. Mark (1915) p. 199 ff., where, following out a hint
by Dr. J. H. Moulton (Zxf 7 xxvi. p. 331 f.), he again lays
the emphasis on the idea of ‘‘ snare” rather than of ‘* stumb-
ling-block.” The etymological connexion of the word with
Skr. skand, ‘‘leap,” ‘‘spirt,”” Lat. scando, makes this
clearer, leading on, as it does, to the Aristophanic use of
σκανδάληθρον for ‘the stick of a mouse-trap”’ (cf. Acharn.
