## πρωτοκαθεδρία.

See *s.v.* πρωτοκλισία.

## πρωτοκλισία.

Like πρωτοκαθεδρία, πρωτοκλισία, "the chief place" at table, appears to be confined to the Gospels and writers who quote them, but for the title πρωτοκλινάρχης (see *l.* in LS⁸), see *Archiv* i. p. 413 f.

## πρωτός.

In the LXX regularly (Thackeray *Gr.* i. p. 183) and in the Κοινή frequently, especially in the Ptolemaic period, πρῶτος stands for πρότερος, an oversetting the usual Hellenistic rule that the comparative does duty for both degrees of comparison. For an ex. almost contemporary with the NT we may cite P Oxy II. 237ᵛⁱ (A.D. 56), where a man asks for information for a supplementary return of lands born since the first or former return for the year had been made—τοῦ τῶν πρώτου ἀπογραφῆς. No utens can, therefore, be laid on the use of πρῶτος in Ac 1ᵃ, as if Luke meant to write a *third* treatise (cf. Ramsay *Paul*, p. 28).

To illustrate Jn 1ᵛˢ, 15ᵛᵛ, we may quote P Leid Wᵛⁱⁱ ⁹ (ii/iii A.D.) (= II. p. 123) ἰδὲ θ᾽ ὁ Φοῖβος ἄντοῦ ἴσχει—πυρ φυσῶν ἄντοῦ νότες τὸν πρῶτον σῖμα, "see thou the first." See further a sacrificial fragment *P. Lond. Inv.* 1219ᵛ, "first." See further a sacrificial fragment in the Loḅan papyrus may have been dependent on Jn 1ᵛᵛ, and translates the latter passage "my Chief" (ib. p. 11 f.), but see Moulton (*Prolog.* pp. 79, 245) in support of the ordinary rendering "before me."

That πρῶτος in Ac 16¹² is not to be understood geographically cf. πρωτόπολις *Philippians* p. 49); but politically has been made clear by Ramsay in *C. and B.* ii. p. 429 (cf. *Paul*, p. 206f.); in connexion with the rivalries of Greek cities to be regarded as "first" or "chief" of their respective districts. For the suggestion that the true reading in *Ac* l.c. is not πρώτη but πρώτης, see *Field Notes*, p. 124, Blass *Philology* p. 87 ff.

In Phil 1. 10ᵛⁱ (A.D. 6 (i.e. = *Chrest. I*. p. 514) a certain Phoenix is described as τοῦ μερισμοῦ τοῦ "one of the first company of E . . ," and the editors compare for a similar military title an unpublished Teubtunis papyrus of B.C. 6 . . . τῶν Μενελάου πρώτων δι τοῦ Ἑρμοκλέους καὶ (ἐπικατηγορημένου) : cf. also *IG* XIV. 601 cited *l.s.* MAὑμ. In P Oxy VIII. 1112² (A.D. 367-70) πρωτι is rendered by the editor "from a few of the first comers," and in *Syll* 523 (= *³* 578)⁸⁵ (ii/B.C.) τὰ πρῶτος ἤρετο is used in the sense of the highest class—class i—in connexion with the attraction of πρώτου (see the editor's note).

The adverbial πρῶτος is born in the soldier's letter to his father BGU II. 423ⁱⁱ (ii/A.D.) (= *Selections*, p. 91) γρά̣φω σοι ἐνταῦθα οὐκ ἐλεήσω σου περὶ τῆς σωτηρίας σου, διότομον πρῶς τὸν θλίβησίν σου, καὶ τὰ πρώτι τοῖς θεοῖς τοῖς ἐνθάδε, "I write therefore first of all to thee . . . in In 12ᵃᵇ Al.), καὶ τὸ πρῶτον ἔμελλεν σαφ. πληρωμα᾽ Ἱεροῖναν (= *Selections*, where the meaning is either "at the first " or "earlier " (= πρότερος), see Olsson *Papyrusbriefe*, p. 53.

For the double superlative πρωτατος cf. P Eleph 10² (B.C. 223-2) ἀπὸ τοῦ πρωτίστου χρόνου, and BGU IV. 1031ⁱⁱⁱ⁵ (ii/A.D.) ἐπὶ πρωτίστου λαμβάνῃ (it. ἐπίστι), and see *Proleg.* p. 236. In BGU III. 830⁸ (i/A.D.) ἐχῖ τὸ πρωτεξ(δ)ε(ίᾳ) (= πρωτεψέλω) Herwerden understands πρωτεψιάς as = "praeciputς," praecipua (?,") but cf. Olsson *Papyrusbriefe* p. 198.

## πρωτοτότης.

For this word which is used metaphorically of a "leader" in *Ac* 24ᵛ and nowhere else in the NT, see Poland *Vereinswesen*, p. 396.

## πρωτότοκος.

"firstborn." An additional proof that this word is to be taken out of the list of purely "Biblical" words, Deissmann (*LAE*, p. 88) cites the undated (apparently early) inscr. *Kaibel* 460⁶ φία τὴν πρωτότοκον ἐκ τέκνωγ[ (= λελ. ληρ[αν]?), "for I see a child by the eyes of the firstborn," and notes that the editor suggests that in the family of the deceased the firstborn always exercised the office of priest. He also refers to a Christian metrical epitaph from Rome of ii/iii A.D. *Kaibel* 730⁶ πρωτότοκον, διετίά, with reference to a firstborn "two-childed" (διετήσας), i.e. child born on a Sunday, who died at the age of two years. Note further a sacrificial decree of *c.* B.C. 200, *Syll* 615 (= *³* 1024)⁵³, in which mention is made of θε τῶν πρωτότοκα animals, and a decree of adoption of A.D. 381, P Lips 28²² (= *Chrest. II*. p. 173) παῖδα τὸ ἐμοῦ τοῦ νἷόν γνήσιον καὶ πρωτότοκον ὡς ἢ ἰδίων αἵματος γεγενῆντο, and the magisl P Oxy I. 1ᵛᵉʳˢᵒ (=*Chrest. I*. p. 144) ἀπὸ πρωτότοκου μηνός.

## πρώτως.

For this *adv.* in Ac 11ᵛⁱ cf. P Par 63ⁱⁱˡ (B.C. 164) (= P Petr III. p. 34) ἔρτ᾽ ἐπ᾽ ἢ πρῶτος παρατασσομένο μεθ᾽ φησιν τότε, "on the understanding that they then be all transferred to the land which provided them first" (Mahaffy). P Strl. II. 235ⁱⁱ (ii/A.D.) Ἀλλ οὖ φαίνεται πρωτος μαρθάνομεν, "this it is not the first time that we learn your beelfeeness " (Edd.), and P Oxy X. 1267²⁷ (registration of a child—A.D. 209) διεδέξατο πρώτος ἀπὸ τοῦ ν(ίοῦ) μηνός τετάρτου τοῦ ν[ί(οῦ) . . μηνός φάος, "we wish that for the first and best henceforth he should be enregistered in the share of a house belonging to me": see the editors' note where πρώτως is taken as meaning that the new owner was making a return for the first time of his new acquisition. Cf. also from the inscrr. *C. and B.* ii. p. 654, No. 569 (i.c. 54-5), and see *Preisigke Fachwörter s.v.*
