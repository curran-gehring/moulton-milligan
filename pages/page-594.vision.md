## ῥίζα.

For the literal sense '' root '' cf. P Oxy XIV. 1674⁴ (iii/a.D.) εἰσενεγκὲ τὴν ῥίζαν τοῦ μετωπίου, with reference to an acacia tree. In P Lond 121²³⁸ (iii/a.D.) (= I. p. 89) the eating of roots of boglion, ῥίζας βοτάνης (l. βρυωνίας), is recommended to prevent the breath from smelling after eating garlic. The metaphorical sense of '' origin,'' '' ancestry,'' is seen in OGIS 383²⁰ (mid. i/B.C.), where Antiochus I. refers to the Persians and Greeks as ἀπὸ γένους εὐτυχεστάτης ῥίζης : cf. Rev 5⁵.

## ῥιζόω.

The rapid transition from the metaphor of '' rooting '' to that of '' building '' in Col 2⁷ is rendered easier, as Lightfoot *ad l.* points out, by the use of ῥίζα in connexion with cities and buildings, e.g. Plut. *Mor.* 321 D περιῤῥεῖ ῥίζαι καὶ καταβαλλόμενα τὴν πόλιν. Similarly with reference to the building of a bridge, *Kaibel* 1078⁵ αἰώνιος ἐῤῥίζωσε.

## ῥιπίζω.

In Hort's *exx. ad* Jas 1⁶ of this word to denote the surface of the water blown upon by shifting breezes, rather than billows lashed by a storm, we may add Aristeas 70 where the workmanship bestowed on certain ornamental basins is described as so life-like that if a breath of wind blew upon them—ῥιπίζοντος τοῦ κατὰ τὸν ἀέρα πνεύματος—the leaves seemed in their places. It should be noted that the verb is derived not from ῥιπή, '' a rushing motion '' (ῥίπτω), but from ῥιπίς, '' a fire-fan.''
