It is doubtful whether the **ἄρχοντες** of v. 19 and the **στρατηγοί** of v. 20 are the same officials according to their Greek or Latin form of designation, or whether the **ἄρχοντες** are the chief magisterial authorities and the **στρατηγοί** the local magistrates of the town. Ramsay (*St. Paul*, p. 217 f.) is inclined to distinguish between them on the ground that a concise writer like Luke would not likely have employed two classes where one was sufficient. He adds that in the case of the Philippian magistrates the title "Praetors" was "not technically accurate," but was employed as a "courtesy title."

The designation **στρατηγός** = *praetor* is of constant occurrence in the papyri, and hardly needs illustration, but see P Oxy II. 294ⁱⁿ (a.d. 22) (= *Selections*, p. 35), BGU IV. 1055¹⁹ (b.c. 13), and P Fay 118¹⁰·¹⁵ (a.d. 110).

## στρατολογέω,

"enlist in the army," in NT only in 2 Tim 2⁴. To the exx. in LS⁹ add Jos. *Bell.* v. 9. 4 βαοθὸν στρατολογήσας.

## στρατοπεδάρχης.

Mommsen (*Untersuch. d. provinzischen Akademie*, 1895, No. XXVII) argues that this word, which occurs in the TR of Ac 28²⁸, means there *princeps peregrinorum*, but in P Lond 196² (c. a.d. 138–161) (= II. p. 152) the editor prefers to understand the title as = *princeps castrorum*, and compares Mommsen *l.c.* p. 458. See also P Iand 6 of a form which is borrowed from Vett. Val. p. 79.¹⁴⁰ τούτων **στρατοπεδάρχης** ναυτικὸν τε καὶ πεξικὸν ἄρχοντα, and *OGIS* 605³ (v/a.d.).

## στρατοπεδάρχης,

"soldiers in camp," "army," as in its only occurrence in the NT (Lk 21²⁰), cf. *Preisigke* 4275⁷ (a.d. 216) Τονλίου Δωρίωνος Σαβίνου τὰ νῦν ἐν **στρατοπέδ**[ῳ] **στρατευόμενος** καὶ similarly 5772⁹ (time of Caracalla). In BGU III. 993¹¹·² (b.c. 128–7) τὸ ἐν τῷ **στρατοπέδῳ** **κατοικα**, "soldiers settled in camp" is rendered by Schubart "an ihrem ordinary meaning "camp."
