## συμπαθέω,

"sharing the experiences" of others (1 Pet 3⁸); cf. *OGIS* 456¹⁶ (B.C. 27–11) τοσαῦτα συμπαθήσας αὐτῷ, P Ryl II. 122²⁴ (A.D. 127) συνπαθήσαντος αὐτοῦ τῇ ἐκείνου (time of Augustus) τῇ τῶν Ἑλλήνων συνπαθίᾳ. In Epictetus συμπαθέω has often the general meaning "correspondence," e.g. *Ep.* i. 463¹¹ (n/i A.D.). See also *Lightfoot* on Heb 4¹⁵, *Symmachus* Psalms *Gr.* p. 226.

## συμφέρω,

From its trans. use "bring together," as in Ac 19¹⁹, *al.*, this verb in the Pauline writings acquires the meaning "to gather together," "be of use," e.g. of marriage union in P Oxy III. 472²⁹ (n/A.D.) συμφεροντος δ' αὐτῶν εἰς μὲν ὑγία, "when these together, may they enjoy health," and so 1 Js 467¹¹ (only Jub.).

The impers. συμφέρει, "it is expedient," is freq. in the NT, e.g. P Oxy III. 47¹·¹⁰ (A.D. 99) συμφέρει γάρ . . . αὐτὸν [ε]ληλυθένα, "for it is an advantage that it should be done," P Oxy III. 471¹⁸ (speech of an advocate—iii/A.D.) συμφέρει το[ῖς] νεωτέροις μᾶλλον ὀρφανίαν, "it is best to acknowledge only the truest fact" (*Edd.*), ib. 532¹⁸, cf. ib. 471¹⁸ συμφέρει σιγάσθαι, "it is inexpedient to keep silence" (*Edd.*), P Ryl II. 244¹⁰ (iii/A.D.) τοῦτο συμφέρω, and ib. 80²⁰ (A.D. 130–8) τὸ συμφέρω this concerns, and *Cf.* ib. 1220⁶ (iii/A.D.) τὰ τούτο συμφέρω. For the constr. of σ. with infin., cf. *Ml. Cr.* p. 134. *Proleg.* p. 210 μὴ ἀναγκαίως δεδε, "this will be of use to prevent their profiting of neglect" (*Edd.*).

For the participle, as in Ac 20²⁰, Heb 12¹⁰, cf. PSI IV. 440¹ᵇ (iii/A.D.) πρὸς τὸ συμφέρον αὐτῶν, and P Amb II. 33¹⁶ (c. B.C. 157) τοῦτον γὰρ γενομένου οὐθὲν τῶν συμφερόντων νεωτεροι, "for if this is done, your interests will not suffer damage" (*Edd.*).

XIII. ⁵, cf. σύμφημι ἐλεήσειεν βαρύτατον μαλακία, "fortune can crush worth, if she comes fraught with suffering" (*Jebb*).

## συμφόρ[α]ζω,

"fellow-countrymen." For the force of this word, which is found only in 1 Thess 4¹⁴, cf. *Milligan Thess. ad l.* and Intr. p. lxii. *Rutherford NP* p. 255 f. illustrates the frequency of similar compounds in late Greek.
